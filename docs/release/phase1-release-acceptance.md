### F-2 - Start-up transient: about 100 MB resident for 2.5 minutes after every start

technical-decisions.md 10.2 states the idle budget as "after startup" without a duration. In steady state the service is well inside it (27.8 MB after a first start, 33.3 MB when configured, 27.2 MB after a restart; `vm-amd64/release.json`, `footprint.idle`), and `REL-FOOT-SETTLE-*` passes. But every start (first start, restart, SIGKILL restart) first holds about 100 MB (100.55 MB peak after the first start, 98.81 MB after a restart) until 145-155 s, i.e. roughly twice the budget for two and a half minutes; the same figures were measured on both hosted CPUs (98-99 MB, settled at 140-160 s). It reproduces natively with the container's environment: with the SMTP relay configured, one heap allocation of about 64 MiB happens 85 ms after start (`GODEBUG=gctrace=1`: `94->94->88 MB`), and Go returns it to the OS only after the runtime's 2-minute forced collection. Not asserted here: which code path allocates it. A follow-up in the product (release the memory after the one-time work) would remove the transient; the harness records the whole curve and gates on the settle. R3 (owner decision): the steady-state definition of the idle budget (the maximum of the samples inside the final 60 s of a 300 s window) is accepted and documented in docs/release-acceptance.md; the transient itself is still reported (`REL-FOOT-SETTLE-*`).

### F-3 - Intermittent fail-closed refusals under the approved workload on the slow VM daemon (R2): cause established, bounded continuity cache measured, drop-rate statistic not met

The adverse runs of the earlier rounds stay sealed (`diagnostics/vm-diag-loop-1.json`, `vm-diag-loop-2.json`, `vm-run-1a01db9-full.json`; SH-2d stays FAIL while their state is open). Established (the root-cause report of round R2/A, `docs/release-acceptance.md`): the fixture daemon saturates under the workload (dockerd and containerd about 165 % and 138 % of one CPU of 4) and the service multiplied its reads (12 per admitted status call, 33 per tail-logs call, every proof drop a failed daemon read with about 50 calls in flight). R2/B changed the product: one epoch-bound continuity check, at most one in flight, valid for less than 1 s, covers read admissions and stream deliveries; mutations, name resolution, target inspects and every authorization decision stay fresh (`docs/docker-backend.md`). The interleaved A/B measurement is sealed in `diagnostics/r2-ab/` (README.md is the guide): clean 10 + 10: metadata reads per call -92.8 %, `/info` per call -99.8 %, daemon CPU per call -86.4 %, p95 status 2.13 s -> 0.48 s and logs 5.99 s -> 0.49 s, 0 refusals/drops/timeouts in 10 B runs; contended (two host CPU burners) 15 A + 30 B: `REL-LOAD-STABLE` failed in 15/15 A runs and 0/30 B runs, timeouts 669 against 0, but the proof dropped in 2/15 A and 0/30 B runs (one-sided Fisher p = 0.106, target p < 0.05): the statistic the plan set for the drop rate alone is **not** met. ### F-4 - Sign-in and setup raise the resident set to about 180 MB (outside the benchmark workload)
