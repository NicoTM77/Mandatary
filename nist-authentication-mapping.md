### 1.2 Interpretation and row format

The PDF labels §§2–5 and Appendix B normative; §§1, 6–8, References, and Appendices A, C–E informative. Capitalized SHALL/SHALL NOT are requirements for a conformance claim; SHOULD/SHOULD NOT are recommendations requiring a considered disposition, not automatic SHALL failures. Informative discussions do not manufacture new normative requirements. The register also records selected MAY branches and non-applicability so absence is visible.[1]

Every design/test row has: **stable row key; exact NIST subsection and strength; requirement; design contract; stable named test(s) with expected outcomes; existing P1 owner task(s); disposition**. Test IDs beginning `N63B4-` are **specifications only, NOT RUN**. A test can be unit, integration, browser, operator-document examination, or third-party evidence review; a simulator is not proof of physical-authenticator or sync-provider internals. All listed product tests must eventually be executed against an exact reviewed commit/build/configuration, with evidence retained by P1.20. A reference to a row in the supplementary clause register incorporates that row's contract, named tests, P1 task and disposition, not a bare assertion of coverage.

Dispositions:

- **P — planned:** compatible with the baseline, still unimplemented/unverified.
- **G — gap/detail:** required contract or evidence missing from the supplied design; resolve before accepting the owning card.
- **D — decision reference:** see the approved D-01–D-10 dispositions in §3; a `D-xx` link does not mean approval is outstanding. Any genuinely new material change still requires Nico, not an autonomous implementation choice.
- **C — conditional/provider/federal:** obligation applies when the stated branch is selected; evidence boundary is explicit.
- **O — unsupported/out of scope:** no implementation; retain a negative capability/claim test and reopen the mapping before enabling it.
- **I — informative:** documented design/risk/UX evaluation, not an invented SHALL.

### 3.1 Phase 1 security-notification assurance contract

The bounded phase amendment is approved. The controls below are Mandatary's delivery/assurance design, not a claim that NIST prescribes SMTP, a particular queue schema or a numeric delivery SLA. NIST's required event/address/repudiation semantics remain the controlling requirements in E03/E08/E15/E16.[1]

1. **Scope and ownership.** Use the already planned SMTP/Submission-over-TLS capability within the Go service and SQLite; no extra service/provider, webhooks or general messaging framework. Notify independently of the browser transaction for authenticator binding/addition/replacement, removal/invalidation, recovery completion, recovery-code replacement/regeneration, and notification-address/security changes affecting these safeguards. Initial code issuance notice is adopted as the recommended safeguard. Notices do not authenticate the recipient, approve access, or substitute for recovery evidence. P1.02 owns address/outbox state; P1.06–09 lifecycle hooks/delivery; P1.16 address and security UX; P1.19/20 isolated SMTP/TLS/failure evidence.
2. **Recipients and assurance.** Support at least two distinct email notification addresses per account; encourage multiple independently controlled destinations without requiring exactly two. At least one confirmed reachable address and configured protected SMTP delivery are prerequisites for ordinary assurance-target activation, including first-admin completion; setup can collect/confirm addresses while remaining setup-only. Do not manufacture a mailbox default. Post-enrollment add/change/remove requires fresh authentication at the account's **maximum available AAL** (AAL2 for any AAL2-capable account), not its weaker current session; retain mandatory WebAuthn-only policy and the D-02 password invariant. Address-control confirmation is purpose/account/address-bound, expiring and one-use; it cannot log in, reset MFA or approve policy. A new address is not active until confirmed. Notify pre-change addresses of proposed/completed changes and confirmed new destinations as applicable; an attacker cannot replace the destination and suppress the old-address notice in one operation. Do not remove the last active destination, restore an old one as already trusted, or change contacts via a recovery-only token. All-factor-loss address correction needs the separately documented host-emergency risk path, cannot be treated as successful AAL2 reauthentication, and retains old-address alerts where available. Email-only Phase 1 does not select postal-only, AAL3, issued-code or identity-proofed recovery profiles; those conditional branches stay unsupported rather than falsely satisfied.
3. **Protected delivery and data minimization.** Require implicit TLS or mandatory STARTTLS with certificate-chain/hostname verification before SMTP authentication or message submission; missing/failed TLS fails closed, never opportunistic plaintext or verification bypass. Sender/relay/recipient configuration is validated against header injection and untrusted destination rewrites. SMTP credentials remain encrypted under TD §6.2 and redacted from all logs/errors. This protects the application→configured relay hop, **not end-to-end mailbox confidentiality or guaranteed human receipt**. Security notices carry minimal event type/time/account reference and clear repudiation/contact instructions; never passwords, setup/recovery codes, seeds, session/bearer tokens, unauthenticated approval links or confidential Docker data. Any service link uses a configured secret-free HTTPS origin. Address-confirmation proof is a separate narrow ceremony, never an authentication/recovery code delivered by the notification feature.
4. **Durable event/outbox boundary.** Commit the lifecycle event and per-recipient outbox records atomically in SQLite; store an event identifier, event time, address-version snapshot, per-recipient outcome/attempt state and deduplication key, with recipient data access-controlled under the approved retention policy. Ordinary binding/replacement, recovery completion and address/security changes that increase or redirect authority cannot report success/unlock if this durable record cannot be committed. A transient relay outage after a successful durable enqueue does not undo a valid security change or erase its notice: retain pending state and surface the outage, without pretending that enqueue means sent. Protective compromise suspension/revocation and session invalidation must **never be delayed or reversed** because SMTP or the outbox is unavailable; if durable revocation itself cannot be written, fail closed on affected credential/session acceptance until repair rather than reporting a durable revoke. Retain a minimal redacted incident/failure marker where storage permits, and expose operator-visible degraded readiness requiring repair. Recovery remains restricted if required address/delivery configuration cannot be established; no email-only bypass.
5. **Failure, retries and restore.** Send to every active notification address, with per-recipient bounded exponential backoff, concurrency/queue bounds and durable retry state across restart. P1.06/19 must record retry intervals, maximum attempts/age, capacity, failure escalation and operator repair/retry procedure before acceptance; Phase 2's request-notification retry count is not a license to drop Phase 1 security notices. Exhaustion/permanent rejection keeps a visible failed/undelivered record and operator escalation; no silent deletion, delivery-success badge or disabled TLS. Redact SMTP diagnostics; mailbox bounce or no confirmed receipt cannot be recorded as human delivery. Persist `pending`, `relay-accepted`, `failed` and any known bounce distinctly; SMTP acceptance is not proof of inbox/human receipt. Snapshot recipient versions so address changes/retry cannot silently retarget old alerts. Treat ambiguous send/ack crashes as at-least-once delivery with stable event identity, not an exactly-once promise. Restore quarantine excludes automatic replay of old outbox work or reactivation of historical addresses; revalidate/reapprove destinations and explicitly classify historical pending events, preserving incident evidence rather than sending stale recovery/security material blindly.
6. **Repudiation and acceptance evidence.** Every notice explains how to dispute the event via the published operator/redress channel; links/contact receipt cannot cancel safeguards, grant sessions or approve policy without required proof. An in-app banner supplements but never replaces independent notices. `N63B4-NOTIFY-DELIVERY` covers all event types, recipient fan-out/partial failures, TLS/auth/certificate errors, restart/retry/exhaustion, send/ack ambiguity, rollback/races, storage failure, protective revocation during outage and secret canaries. `N63B4-NOTIFY-ADDRESSES` covers ≥2 destinations, initial confirmation, old/new-address alerts, stale/replayed/wrong-purpose proofs, weaker-session rejection, last-address removal, recovery/restore and configured-contact repudiation. `N63B4-TLS-BOUNDARY`, `N63B4-RECOVERY-NO-SESSION` and `N63B4-RESTORE-AUTH-STATE` repeat the integrated safeguards. **All acceptance specifications are NOT RUN.**
